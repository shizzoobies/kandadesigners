# AI Integration Membership
## Business Plan, K&A Performance LLC

---

## 1. What it is

A recurring coaching membership that gets small business owners actually using AI in their operations, delivered by someone who has shipped it. Not a course. Not a Slack group. A named person who knows their business, on a call and on email, working on their real problems.

**Positioning line:** Get AI working inside your business, with the person who builds them.

**Who it is for:** owner-operators and small teams, roughly 1 to 15 employees, already spending money on marketing and therefore already comfortable with a monthly line item. Service businesses first: clinics, contractors, salons, venues, agencies. These are the people Synovial already sells to and the people K&A already builds for.

**Who it is not for:** hobbyists, career-changers, people who want to "learn AI." They churn in month three and they cost the same to serve.

**What the member gets**

- Onboarding session: goals, current stack, an AI opportunity map for their business
- Their own Claude subscription, set up on the first call, on their card and under their name
- Monthly (or weekly) working video call on their actual projects
- Direct email access to Alex for questions between calls
- Custom builds and configurations walked through live: sites, Drive and email automation, document workflows, internal assistants, whatever their business needs
- Everything they build stays in their account and belongs to them

**What K&A does not provide:** Claude seats, accounts, credentials, or usage on anyone's behalf. Anthropic's terms prohibit reselling or intermediating Claude usage, and the entire business account is not worth risking on it. This is also a selling point: "you own your account and everything in it" matches how K&A already sells websites.

## 2. Tiers and pricing

**The lead offer: the 90-Day AI Launch. $2,000.**

One price, one outcome, a defined end date. Twelve weeks, weekly working sessions, and the member finishes with something running in their business that was not there before: their site, their document and Drive workflow, their internal assistant, their intake or quoting process. Their account, their data, their build.

The arc:

- **Weeks 1-2.** Opportunity map of their business. Account setup on their own subscription. One quick win shipped in the first session so they feel it immediately.
- **Weeks 3-8.** Build the highest-value workflow together, live, on their real data.
- **Weeks 9-12.** They build one themselves with Alex watching. Handoff doc, recorded walkthroughs, and a written plan for what comes next.

Why this leads: a one-time price with a named deliverable is a far easier yes than a subscription, it produces the testimonial that sells the next three, and it gives Synovial a real invoice to sell instead of a $350 one. Payment can split into three monthly payments of $700 for businesses that need it, same as K&A does on builds.

**Launch + Site. $3,999.**

The same twelve weeks, with a full custom site built alongside it. This is the $2,500 rebuild tier plus the $2,000 Launch, with $500 off for taking both.

The site is built by K&A at K&A's normal pace and normal standard. It is not co-built. A site is a once-in-five-years artifact, so teaching someone to build one burns twelve weeks on a skill they will never use again, and a site a novice had their hands in is one that cannot be stood behind on accessibility or quality.

What gets taught instead is the layer they will operate every week: updating their own copy, handling the inquiries the site generates, writing service pages and posts, reading what is working. Weekly sessions still walk them through what was built and why, and hand over the controls for the parts they maintain. Show, do not co-build. Clients asking to be walked through it want to understand it, not to do it.

The $5,000 logo-and-photography tier and the $8,000 AI-integration build stay separate and quoted by scope at the member rate. Those carry real costs and do not belong in a bundle.

**Added 2026-08-29: Build Alongside, from $10,000, scoped per project.** The co-built tier the $3,999 bundle deliberately is not. At this price the twelve-weeks-of-attention math changes: Alex still authors the site to the studio standard, but the client is in the room for every session, learning AI use, building, and API integration on their own real project. Placed at the bottom of the offer stack on the page, sold quietly, taken by the few who want to own the machine and understand it.

**The back end: membership.** Offered in week 10, when they can see what they would lose by stopping. Two tiers.

| | Core | Advisory |
|---|---|---|
| Video calls | 1 per month, 45 min | 1 per week, 45 min |
| Email support | Yes, 1 business day | Yes, priority |
| Retail price | $350/mo | $999/mo |
| Synovial (20%) | $70/mo | $200/mo |
| K&A take | $280/mo | $799/mo |
| Seats available | 40 | 8 |

**Why Advisory is $999 and not $400.** At $250 for one call and $400 for four, the expensive tier is the better deal per hour, which means everyone buys it and it is the tier that eats the most time. $999 for four calls is $250 per call against $350 at Core. Still a real discount for committing, without inverting the value.

**The membership is the back end, not the front end.** Nobody who has never worked with you subscribes to a monthly call. They buy a result. So the Launch Program is what gets sold, and the membership is what they roll into when it ends.

## 3. The Synovial channel

**Structure: K&A is the seller of record. Synovial earns 20% of retail, paid on the Launch Program and on all recurring revenue for as long as the member stays.**

| | Retail | Synovial (20%) | K&A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90-Day AI Launch | $2,000 one-time | $400 | $1,600 |
| Launch + Site | $3,999 one-time | $800 | $3,199 |
| Core | $350/mo | $70/mo | $280/mo |
| Advisory | $999/mo | $200/mo | $799/mo |

K&A contracts the member, bills the member, and pays Synovial commission monthly on collected revenue. Hannah pitches, books the intro call, and gets paid on everything that follows.

Expect Launch + Site to become the tier she leads with, because $800 is worth chasing in a way $400 is not. That is the right outcome: it is the tier that pulls K&A's build business along with it.

Paying commission on renewals, not just the first sale, is what makes this worth her time. One Launch that converts to 18 months of Core pays her $1,660. That is the number to put in front of her, not the $400.

**Terms to put in writing before the first sale**

1. **Billing.** K&A bills the member directly and pays Synovial commission monthly on collected revenue, due by the 10th for the prior month. Commission is earned on cash collected, not on signed contracts, so a member who defaults does not owe commission. Send her a monthly statement without being asked.
2. **One retail price.** K&A does not sell direct below Synovial's price. If K&A closes someone independently, K&A keeps the full amount at the same public price. No undercutting the channel, ever, or the partnership dies in month four.
3. **Attribution window.** A member counts as Synovial's for as long as they remain a paying member on the tier she sold, on business she introduced. Renewals pay commission. If she stops selling, trailing commissions continue on her existing book for 12 months, then stop.
4. **Non-exclusive both ways.** K&A can sell direct and through other agencies. Synovial can sell other AI offerings. Revisit if volume justifies exclusivity.
5. **Non-solicit and no white-label.** Synovial does not rebrand this as her own service, and does not hire around it. The member knows Alex is delivering.
6. **Termination.** Either side out on 30 days notice. Existing members are served through their paid term regardless.
7. **What she sells and what she does not.** She sells outcomes and books the intro call. She does not scope technical work, promise features, or quote custom builds. Every technical question routes to Alex.

**Hannah on the K&A Team org.** She is already inside the operation: K&A runs the partnership GitHub and Cloudflare, holds admin on the shared client stack, and maintains her site. A seat for a collaborator doing shared client work is ordinary organizational use, and Team is where the value actually is for that setup: shared projects, org-wide skills deployment, consistent branding assets across builds, and no model training on client content by default.

The line to hold is the one that made the member seats a problem. Her seat exists because she does work with K&A, not as a benefit bundled into what she sells. It never gets offered to a prospect, mentioned in the pitch, or extended to a Synovial client. If she starts running unrelated Synovial-only client work through it at volume, that is the point to have her buy her own.

**Separately, make her member zero.** Not for compliance reasons, for sales reasons. Run her through the full Core onboarding on a member's footing so she has felt the buying flow, the setup call, and the first quick win. A seat on the org teaches her the tool. Member zero teaches her the product she is selling.

**Enablement she actually needs:** a one-page sell sheet, three specific before-and-after stories from existing K&A clients, an objection sheet ("can't I just use ChatGPT for free"), and a calendar link. Without these she will not sell it, no matter how good the margin is.

## 4. Capacity and unit economics

**The binding constraint is not the calendar.** Scheduling flexibility means call slots are not what limits the roster. What limits it is depth of attention: remembering 40 businesses well enough that every call picks up where the last one left off, plus email turnaround, plus the K&A build work that pays more per hour than any membership seat does.

**Caps for year one:** 40 Core, 8 Advisory. That is 72 calls a month, roughly 18 a week, plus email. Advisory is the tier that needs a real ceiling, because weekly cadence with 8 members is already 32 calls before a single Core call happens. Publish the Advisory cap. Scarcity on the high tier is true and it sells.

**Monthly economics at that cap**

- Revenue to K&A: 40 × $280 + 8 × $799 = $17,592/mo
- Direct costs: video conferencing, scheduling, member portal hosting, own Claude plan and Hannah's seat. Under $350/mo
- Gross margin: roughly 98%. There is no COGS once member seats are off the table
- Time: about 60 hours of calls per month plus email

That ceiling is a ceiling, not a forecast. Reaching it means Synovial closing four a month for a year straight. Plan on the ramp below and treat the cap as the number that tells you when to stop selling or raise prices.

**Break-even:** month one. There is no meaningful startup cost.

**Realistic ramp, launch-led**

Assumes 2 to 3 Launches sold per month once Synovial is enabled, roughly a third of them taking the site bundle, and 60% converting to membership at the end of the 90 days.

| | Activity | K&A revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1-3 | 6 Launches, 2 with site | $4,300/mo |
| Month 4-6 | 8 more Launches, first 4 members convert | $5,700/mo |
| Month 7-12 | 3 Launches/mo, 15 to 20 members on the books | $11,000 to $13,000/mo |

Launch revenue is lumpy and front-loaded, membership revenue compounds behind it. By month 12 the recurring base should be approaching the Launch revenue, which is the whole point of leading with the Launch.

**Watch the build load.** Every Launch + Site adds one to two weeks of solo build time on top of the sessions. Two site bundles running at once alongside existing K&A client work is the practical ceiling. If Hannah sells three in a month, the third one starts the following month and gets told so honestly at signing.

The first two or three Launches should come from existing K&A clients at a discount in exchange for a filmed testimonial and permission to name them. Fore Motion Golf, EFPD, or David's BBQ are the obvious candidates: local, recognizable, and already trusting the work.

## 5. Delivery

**Standardize the back end, customize the front end.** Every member's content is different, the structure is not.

- **Month 1:** opportunity map, account setup, one quick win shipped in the first session
- **Month 2:** their highest-value workflow, built live
- **Month 3:** they build one themselves with Alex watching
- **Ongoing:** whatever they bring, plus a monthly "here is what changed" note to the whole roster

**Assets to build once and reuse:** the opportunity map template, a plan-selection guide, five or six recorded walkthroughs of the most common asks (Drive organization, email drafting, document workflows, site edits), and a starter skills and project pack members can drop into their own account. Every hour spent on reusable assets buys back call time later.

**Retention mechanic:** the monthly roster note and the recorded library are what keep someone paying in month eight when they have run out of questions.

## 6. Risks

- **Churn at month three.** The most likely failure mode. Mitigation: the Launch Program arc, the asset library, and quarterly goal resets so there is always a next thing.
- **Roster depth, not calendar.** Scheduling is flexible, so the failure mode is not missed calls, it is 40 members getting shallower attention as the roster fills while K&A builds compete for the same head. Mitigation: hard caps, a written one-page state-of-play per member updated after every call, and a standing rule that Advisory members are never rescheduled for build work.
- **Channel dependency.** If Synovial is the only source of members, she effectively owns the business. Mitigation: direct sales run in parallel from day one, non-exclusive terms, K&A holds the billing relationship.
- **Scope creep into free consulting.** "While we're on here, can you just build me a page." Mitigation: written scope. Builds are quoted separately at K&A rates. Members get a discount on builds, not free builds. This is a feature, not a problem: the membership becomes the top of the funnel for $2,500 to $8,000 site work.
- **Terms compliance.** Never provision, pay for, or intermediate Claude access for a member. No exceptions, no favors, not even a trial.
- **Employer conflict.** Confirm the U-Haul side-business position covers a recurring coaching product, not just project work, before this is public.
