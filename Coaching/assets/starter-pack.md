# Starter Pack: Setting Up Your Own Claude Account

Walked through together in session one. Everything here happens in your account, on your subscription, under your login. Alex guides, you drive. By the end of the hour you'll have a working setup and one real task shipped through it.

---

## 1. Your account

- Subscription created on your own card, under your own name, billed by Anthropic directly.
- You hold the login. Nobody else needs it, including Alex. In sessions you share your screen, you type.
- Why it works this way: everything you build belongs to you. If you stopped working with K&A tomorrow, all of it keeps running.

## 2. Projects: one per job, not one big bucket

A project is a workspace with a memory: a description of your business plus the files you give it. Set up two or three today, not ten.

Good first projects for an owner-operator:

- **[Your Business] HQ**: the general one. Business description, your services, your voice.
- **Quotes and Estimates**: your price list, two or three past quotes you were happy with.
- **Customer Emails**: a handful of your best sent replies, so it learns how you talk.

Start every work conversation inside the right project. That's the whole habit.

## 3. Writing the project description

This is the highest-leverage ten minutes of the setup. Answer these, in plain sentences, and paste it into the project description:

1. What the business does and for whom. ("We're a two-truck plumbing company serving Gainesville and Alachua County, mostly residential repair and remodels.")
2. Who's writing. ("I'm the owner. I write like I talk: direct, friendly, no corporate filler.")
3. What you charge and how. (Hourly, flat-rate, deposit terms, whatever's stable.)
4. Rules that never change. ("Never promise a same-day appointment. Always mention we're licensed and insured. Prices are estimates until we see the job.")
5. What good output looks like. ("Keep emails under 150 words. Quotes get a line-item table.")

Then add files: your service list, a price sheet, a few examples of your best work. Real material beats descriptions of it.

## 4. Five prompt patterns that earn their keep

Tuned for owner-operator service businesses. Save these where you can grab them (see section 5).

**Quoting**
> "Here are my job notes from today's site visit: [paste notes]. Draft a quote using my price sheet. Line-item table, plain-language scope description, my standard terms at the bottom. Flag anything in my notes you didn't have a price for."

**Inbox triage**
> "Here are today's unread emails: [paste]. Sort them into: needs my reply today, can wait, quick yes/no, junk. For the quick ones, draft the reply in my voice so I can send with one edit."

**Document drafting**
> "Draft a [contract addendum / follow-up letter / warranty explanation] for [customer] covering [the situation in two sentences]. Match the tone of the examples in this project. Give me a version to react to, not a questionnaire."

**Meeting and call notes**
> "Here are my rough notes from [the call]: [paste]. Turn them into: decisions made, who owes what by when, and a short recap email I can send to everyone on it."

**Writing service pages**
> "Write a service page about [service] for my site. Who it's for, what's included, what it costs or how pricing works, why us, and a clear next step. Use my business description. No hype words, write like I'd say it across a counter."

The pattern behind all five: give it your real material, tell it the format you want back, and ask it to flag what it's unsure about instead of guessing.

## 5. Habits that stick

- **Keep your prompts somewhere you'll actually look.** A doc pinned in your Drive called "AI Prompts" is fine. Copy in anything that worked. Your library, in your account.
- **When to reach for it:** any time you're about to type more than a paragraph, re-explain something you've explained before, or push a task to the evening. Those are the tells.
- **When not to:** final numbers, legal commitments, and anything you'd want a professional to check. Draft with it, decide yourself.
- **Fresh chat for a fresh task.** Long wandering conversations drift. New task, new conversation, same project.
- **Feed the project as you go.** New price sheet, new service, a reply you were proud of: drop it into the project files. The setup gets better every week you use it, and it's all yours.
