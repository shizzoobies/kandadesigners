## Partner sell sheet for Synovial Marketing

---

### The one-sentence version

Ninety days of weekly working sessions with Alex, ending with one real workflow in the client's business running on AI, built in the client's own account.

---

### Who to pitch

Owner-operators, 1 to 15 employees, service businesses. Clinics, trades, venues, salons, shops, agencies. The tell is an owner who says some version of "I spend my whole evening on quotes" or "I can't keep up with the inbox."

Your existing book is the entire target list. Anyone already paying you monthly for marketing has already decided that outside help is worth a line item.

**Skip:** hobbyists, people who want to "learn AI," anyone shopping for a certificate, and anyone who can't commit an hour a week.

---

### How to open it

Don't lead with AI. Lead with the hours.

> "What's the thing that eats your week that you know shouldn't take that long?"

Let them answer, then:

> "I work with a guy who builds that away. Twelve weeks, weekly sessions, you end up owning the thing and knowing how to run it. It's two grand. Want me to set up twenty minutes with him?"

That's the whole pitch. Your job is the introduction, not the scope.

---

### What you sell and what you don't

**You sell:** the outcome, the price, the intro call.

**You don't:** scope the technical work, promise a specific feature, quote custom builds, or answer "can it do X." Every one of those routes to Alex. Say "good question, that's exactly what the call is for."

Overpromising on the sale is the fastest way to a refund and a bad review, and it costs you the recurring commission that makes this worth doing.

### Objections

**"Can't I just use ChatGPT for free?"**
You can, and some people do. What they're paying for is not access to the tool, it's twelve weeks not spent finding out which approaches were dead ends. Alex has already spent them.

**"Two thousand dollars is a lot."**
It's three payments of $700, and it's a one-time cost for something that keeps working after. Compare it to what one part-time hire costs for a month.

**"I'm not technical."**
Neither are most of the clients. The last three weeks of the program exist specifically to make sure they can run it without him.

**"Is there a monthly fee too?"**
They'll need their own Claude subscription, about $20 a month, paid to Anthropic on their own card. That's it. Alex sets it up with them on the first call.

**"Why can't he just give me access to his?"**
Because then it isn't theirs. Everything they build lives in their own account. If they walk away from Alex tomorrow, it all keeps running. That's a feature, and it's the same way K&A hands over code on every website build.

**"Do I get to build the site myself?"**
No, and that's deliberate. Alex builds it to K&A's standard, accessibility included, because a site is something they'll replace once in five years. What the sessions teach is the part they touch every week: updating their own copy, handling the inquiries it brings in, writing new pages. They see every decision, they just don't do the wiring.

**"What if it doesn't work for my business?"**
The first call is twenty minutes and Alex will tell them if there's nothing worth building. He'd rather say no than take two grand for a bad fit.
