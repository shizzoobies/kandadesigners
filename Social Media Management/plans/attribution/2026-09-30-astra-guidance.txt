
Fable pipeline attribution
implementation guidance
For the agent running the K&A social and site pipeline | 30 September 2026
Design a reusable measurement layer for all existing and future sites, landing pages, forms, funnels and social campaigns built through this pipeline. Connect published content to observed enquiries, qualified leads, bookings, won work and actual paid revenue where the evidence supports that connection. Propose K&A as the first test site, then make adoption configurable for each client.
Start with a plan and an approval gate
Read the repositories, AGENTS instructions and existing work first. Return an architecture and staged change plan before implementing. This brief does not authorize activating tracking, deploying, creating credentials, expanding permissions, sending customer data to platforms or performing destructive database migrations. Identify each action that needs approval, including its site, account, data fields, destination and impact. Use synthetic data for the proposed staging tests.

1  Build one reusable layer
Keep the core independent of a particular framework, form provider, CRM or social platform. Expose a small site integration contract and provider adapters. Use shared code with explicit per-client configuration; do not copy K&A account IDs or tracking snippets into every generated site.
Per client configuration and isolation
Identity and policy. Define stable client_id and site_id; approved domains; owner; environment; timezone; currency; enabled channels; publishing policy; consent categories; attribution rules; retention; and sensitivity restrictions. Keep platform accounts and secret references separate per tenant and environment.
Security. Derive tenant identity from trusted server configuration or authenticated access. Never trust a browser-supplied tenant ID alone. Enforce tenant scoping on writes, reads, exports and background jobs. Restrict admin access, redact logs, keep credentials server-side and add a per-site tracking kill switch. New adapters start disabled.
Durability. Store campaign and publication records outside disposable PostDesk state. Import existing manifests idempotently. Retain native post IDs, Metricool IDs, actual published URLs and publication times; reconcile scheduled, failed, deleted and published states without creating duplicate posts.
A link contract that can identify a post
Normalize source values by network, including facebook, instagram, linkedin and youtube. Keep source, medium and campaign semantics stable. Use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, with utm_id for the stable campaign ID and utm_content for an opaque post or creative placement ID. Keep paid and organic traffic distinguishable. Never put names, emails, phone numbers or customer IDs in URLs. [1]
K&A’s current planner already adds source=network, medium=social and campaign=date-postsuffix. Verify these become actual utm_* parameters in the final destination URL. Extend the existing builder with a documented mapping and backward compatibility; do not silently rename historical campaigns.
The kaperformancefl SmartLink, ID 169122, currently gives website buttons Instagram/smartlink tags without a post ID. Use network-specific entry links and placement IDs where supported. A shared bio link supports placement-level attribution unless a real post link can be established. Do not reuse an Instagram source tag across networks or invent post credit; resolve the YouTube placeholder before using it.
Preserve eligible touches without inventing history
Subject to each site’s approved consent and privacy policy, record the first eligible touch and latest eligible non-direct touch: normalized UTMs, permitted click IDs, sanitized landing URL, occurrence time and a pseudonymous browser ID. A later direct visit must not erase the prior eligible touch. Define precedence, expiry, cross-domain behavior and policy version explicitly. Click IDs are personal data; browser identifiers and raw URLs need the same care.
Snapshot eligible attribution when the lead is created. A later sale linked to that known lead can retain its source after the browser cookie expires, subject to retention and attribution policy. Cookie expiry, internal attribution eligibility and platform lookback limits are separate settings. Anonymous cross-device journeys, deleted cookies and organic impressions cannot guarantee identification of a later buyer.

2  Define the durable data contract
Propose the storage location after inspecting the existing stack. A central reporting service with tenant-scoped records is appropriate if isolation can be enforced. Keep transactional contact details in an access-controlled lead store and expose only the minimum fields needed for reporting.
Conceptual records
Campaign and publication. client_id, site_id, campaign_id, post_id, placement_id, network, organic_or_paid, Metricool brand/post IDs, native account/post IDs, destination URL, published URL, scheduled_at, published_at, status and manifest reference.
Touch and attribution. touch_id, tenant/site keys, permitted browser reference, occurred_at, normalized UTM fields, permitted click IDs, sanitized landing page, consent state/version and attribution_policy_version. Preserve first and last eligible touch references plus an explicit unknown reason.
Lead and lifecycle event. lead_id, event_id, tenant/site keys, form_id, event_type, occurred_at, received_at, source system, source record ID, optional order/deal ID, attribution snapshot, consent provenance and deduplication key. Lifecycle events include submitted, qualified, booked, won, payment_received and refund, as each client actually uses them.
Money and delivery. For financial events include transaction_id, amount in minor units, currency, actual payment/refund time and related lead/deal ID. Keep won value separate from cash received. For each external delivery retain destination, event mapping, attempts, receipt/status, last error and next retry. Store no credentials or raw form text in these records.
Make the server the conversion authority
Inventory every enquiry path, including standard forms, modal forms, chat, courses and third-party bookings. Route successful submissions through an approved server endpoint or a verified provider webhook. Validate input and abuse controls, persist the lead and one submission event, then acknowledge success. Use a transaction plus an outbox, or an equivalent durable design, so email and adapter deliveries can retry independently.
Keep the current notification emails working. Distinguish an accepted lead from a failed notification delivery. A click on Submit, validation failure or rejected submission is not a conversion. A stable idempotency key must make double clicks and retries resolve to the same submission event. Where a browser event is enabled, return the server event ID so browser and server refer to the same real conversion.
Client-side analytics failure must not erase an accepted lead. If tracking JavaScript is blocked but submission succeeds, the backend event must persist. Handle completely disabled JavaScript through a supported form fallback or explicitly document the form limitation.
Connect sales without fabricating revenue
Choose a verified live CRM, booking or payment source per client. Support direct checkout purchases without inventing a prior lead. Link changes by stable lead/deal/transaction IDs, with a minimal audited manual entry or CSV import option where no integration exists. Imports and webhooks must be idempotent. Record corrections and refunds explicitly; never count won work and its later payment as two sales or treat an invoice as proof of payment.
Use actual event time and retain receipt time separately. A sale occurring months after an enquiry differs from an old sale uploaded late. Preserve the internal truth even if an external platform rejects it as outside its limits; never backdate an event to make an API accept it.

3  Add approved adapters and useful reporting
Make external tracking optional per site
Before enabling an adapter, verify account ownership, consent requirements, permitted event fields, platform eligibility, access and current API documentation. Present new credentials, permission expansion, terms and customer-data transfers for approval. Do not assume a working publishing connection grants conversion upload or analytics access.
Meta. Where approved, combine Pixel and Conversions API with one event ID and matching event name for the same event. Confirm the current deduplication rules and prove the result in Events Manager. Keep the internal ledger authoritative even when browser or platform delivery fails.
LinkedIn. Where approved, use Insight Tag and Conversions API with shared eventId deduplication and the correct ad account/conversion mapping. Check each conversion category’s current window and API permissions. [4, 5]
Google and YouTube. Use approved GA4 measurement and Google Ads conversion configuration for paid YouTube or other Google campaigns. For new offline implementations, assess the Data Manager API against current Google access/deprecation rules. Do not assume a legacy upload endpoint is available. Paid engaged-view claims are a platform metric; ordinary organic video views do not identify later customers. [6, 7]
Window handling. Keep an explicit per-platform configuration for click/view lookbacks, counting rules and upload-age limits. Verify these at implementation time rather than hardcoding one universal number. Display the model and window used alongside each platform’s results. [4, 8]
Consent and sensitive clients are release gates
Separate necessary enquiry processing from optional analytics and advertising. Record the permitted purposes and notice version, gate browser tags and server sends consistently, and implement withdrawal, deletion and expiry across identifiers, queues and downstream processes. Do not interpret denied consent as permission for a server-side tracking workaround.
Use field allowlists and sanitized URLs. Do not send form free text, health details or unrelated contact data to ad platforms. Hashing does not remove sensitivity. For healthcare and other sensitive client sites, block advertising tags and identity uploads until a page-level and data-level policy review establishes an allowed configuration; some uses remain prohibited. [9, 10]
Dashboard requirements
Provide client/site, date, network, paid/organic, campaign, post/placement and attribution-model filters. Show observed accepted leads, qualified/booked/won counts, distinct customers where legitimately known, paid revenue by currency, refunds, conversion rates with stated denominators, and lead-to-sale delay. Support conversion-date views and acquisition cohorts so a late sale can be traced to its original campaign.
Keep deduplicated internal outcomes separate from platform-reported conversions, modeled results and view-through claims. Never sum overlapping platform claims into total customers or revenue. Import social metrics with their source, metric definition, reporting interval and freshness; do not sum cumulative snapshots.
Show attribution coverage and explicit missing-data states: not connected, not authorized, not collected, pending, stale, error or not applicable. Missing revenue feeds must not display invented zeros. Include last successful import, rejected events, duplicates, unmatched leads, consent coverage and delivery health. Metricool can build UTM links but does not analyze UTM attribution itself. [2]

4  Roll out in achievable stages
Keep each stage reviewable. K&A is the proposed pilot, not the limit of the design. Do not move from planning into implementation, or from staging into production, without the relevant approval.
Stage 0  Inspect and propose. Return the repository/deployment map, full form inventory, per-client integration matrix, existing-data reconciliation, architecture, schema/migrations, privacy choices, costs and approval list. Identify unknowns and preserve the existing publishing workflow.
Stage 1  Build the internal foundation after approval. Add the shared configuration contract, stable link IDs, durable campaign import, accepted-lead ledger, email-safe submission path and a minimal internal dashboard. Use isolated staging storage, synthetic fixtures and disabled external adapters. Prefer additive migrations with backup and rollback plans.
Stage 2  Prove lifecycle reporting. Connect an approved source of qualified, booked, won and paid events, or use a controlled import. Demonstrate a delayed sale, retries, refunds and attribution coverage. Release the internal reporting slice before optional ad-platform adapters if that is the fastest reliable path.
Stage 3  Validate optional adapters. For each approved destination, use its supported test mode and synthetic events, confirm receipt and deduplication, record API limits and inspect consent behavior. Verify Metricool’s API entitlement and permitted endpoints before building an importer; MCP access alone does not establish Advanced/Custom API entitlement. [3]
Stage 4  Approve the pilot and generalize. Present the exact production change, affected site/account, payloads, migration, rollback and diagnostics evidence. After approval, release K&A, verify real deployment behavior and reconcile permitted test outcomes end to end. Then onboard each client through the same checklist with its own accounts, consent policy and publishing permissions. Add the contract and checks to site-generation templates so future builds inherit the capability with tracking disabled until approved.
Acceptance tests required before production
Links and delayed return. Test Facebook/Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube destination tags and stable IDs through redirects and supported link placements. Return directly later and confirm the eligible source survives. Test absent tags, expired identifiers and unattributable journeys without guessed credit.
Submission correctness. Validation failure and provider/backend rejection produce no accepted conversion. Double clicks, retries and webhook replays produce one logical event. Accepted submissions persist with analytics JavaScript blocked. Notification email still sends or enters a visible retry state.
Privacy and isolation. Denied or withdrawn consent blocks unauthorized tags and queued sends. Deletion/expiry removes or de-identifies the approved scope. Attempt cross-tenant reads, writes, exports and account-routing mistakes; all must fail safely.
Durability and revenue. Remove disposable PostDesk test rows and verify campaigns and attribution remain. Replay won/payment imports without inflating totals. Apply refunds once. Simulate a sale after cookie expiry using a known lead and an event too old for external upload; keep correct internal records and surface the rejection.
Reporting and release. Test disconnected/stale/error feeds, attribution coverage, timezone boundaries, currency separation and cumulative metric imports. Prove browser/server deduplication in approved platform diagnostics. Document feature-flag rollback and require explicit production signoff.
