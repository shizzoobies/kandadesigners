## The shape of a day

- **Lead, 10:30 AM** (weekends 12:00 PM): a carousel, or a reel built from real clinic photos
  with text beats. Facebook and Instagram.
- **Card, 5:30 PM**: one idea, one frame, feed size. Facebook and Instagram.
- Every frame carries the EFPD logo and familypracticedirect.com. Every caption ends on how to
  book: call 941 417 7386, or the patient portal for existing patients.
- Facebook captions put the tagged link on line 2. Instagram says link in bio (the bio already
  links to the site) and carries the hashtags in the first comment.
- Reels run muted-first: on-screen text plus a music bed, no narration. A track never repeats
  within 30 days. This client's music history is empty, so the build brief assigns fresh tracks.
- Nothing is scheduled in Metricool (brand 7196744) until the desk shows it approved.

## Why this mix

- **New patients first.** The site is three weeks old and the Instagram has 224 followers.
  Week 1 answers the three things a new patient checks before calling: who you are, where you
  are, how to get seen. Week 2 shows what you treat.
- **Three ways to be seen, said plainly.** Insurance accepted, uninsured patients welcome, and a
  DPC membership for people who want the flat-fee model. The site says all three; most practices
  say one. That is the differentiator, so it gets its own carousel in week 1 and the DPC
  explainer stays prices-free until the practice confirms current rates.
- **A real review every other evening.** The five Google reviews published on the site, quoted
  whole, first name and initial as shown there. They carry the trust that a three-week-old site
  cannot.
- **The providers, one at a time.** Dr. Kulawik, David Hervig and Julie Vera Rivas each get a
  card: name, credentials, role, as the live site states them, nothing from the draft bios that
  are still pending approval.
- **October is on our side.** Flu shots, and breast cancer awareness month on Tue 10/13, both
  general and sourced.
- **No one is in the photos.** The clinic set includes at least one real patient, so builders use
  only the sign and rooms with nobody in them. HIPAA has no gray area here.

## What the posts never say (healthcare rules plus the site's own notes)

- Anything about a patient, or any image with a patient in it.
- Any outcome, guarantee, "best", "#1", or before-and-after. Nothing on weight results.
- Personal medical advice. Education posts carry: "General information, not medical advice. Call
  the office with questions, or 911 in an emergency."
- Prescription drug names, unless the practice confirms them.
- Membership prices (the $99 and $175 figures on the site date from 2016 and are unconfirmed).
- A combined years-of-experience figure.
- Anything about the chiropractor who shares the office.
- "Same-day" as a promise for insurance patients. Same or next day access is a stated DPC
  membership benefit; everyone else is "call the office".
- The door sign's phone number (7586). The site's number is 941 417 7386.
- Em dashes. Decorative numbering, arrows on every button, repeated layouts: the client's own
  visual standards ban these as AI tells.
