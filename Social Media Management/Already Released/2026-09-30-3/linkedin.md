If everyone passes your quiz, it measured nothing.

A quiz that everyone aces tells you people can read. It does not tell you whether they can do the job, and that is the whole reason the assessment exists.

Five fixes we use on every course, each shown in this document as a weak and a strong example question:

1. Test what they would do, not what they can recite. Put the learner in a scenario and ask for the next move.
2. Make every wrong answer tempting to someone. A distractor nobody picks is a free point.
3. Retire "All of the above." It rewards guessing, not knowing.
4. Ask it straight. Trick negatives test reading, not judgment.
5. Write feedback that teaches the why. "Incorrect, the answer is B" is a missed lesson.

The example questions are ours, written for a customer service course.

The takeaway: take one recall question from your current quiz and rewrite it as a short scenario with a decision at the end. Then give each wrong answer feedback that explains why it is wrong.
