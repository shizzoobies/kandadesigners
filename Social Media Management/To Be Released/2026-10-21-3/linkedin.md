"Incorrect." tells a learner they missed. It doesn't tell them what to do next.

Five rules for feedback that teaches, one per page in this document, each shown on a real wrong answer in one of our sample courses:

1. Name what went wrong. Not just that it is wrong, but which part. In our RFI course, a learner who files a complaint under References and a paint question under Proposal gets "Needs another pass: References, Proposal." They fix the two fields, not the whole request.

2. Point at the rule. Send them back to the idea that decides the answer. In our strength course, a task placed on the wrong movement station gets "Notice whether the task lowers and rises, moves mainly at the hips, pushes, pulls or travels with a load." The next try comes from the rule, not a guess.

3. Show the right move. When the method is the lesson, show the working. Our P&L course answers a wrong operating income with the subtraction that gets to the right one.

4. Let them retry. Mark the miss, leave it open, and ask for another go. Our safety course says "Revisit this placement. Read the situation and try a different control," and saves the full explanation for when they get it.

5. Keep it short. What to try, then why. Our nutrition course does it in three short sentences.

The last page is the five rules on one board, to check against the next quiz you write.
