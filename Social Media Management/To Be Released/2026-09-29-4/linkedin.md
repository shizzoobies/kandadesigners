Most courses start in the wrong place: with everything people should know, followed by a quiz.

Cathy Moore's Action Mapping flips the order:

1. Start with a measurable business goal. For example: cut lifting injuries 10% this year. (That's an example goal, not a statistic.)
2. Ask what people need to do on the job to reach it.
3. Design practice that looks like the real task. "A box might be too heavy to lift and no one's around. What do you do?" If they lift it anyway, don't tell them it's wrong. Show the consequence: a sharp pain in the lower back.
4. Only then, decide what they truly need to know. Usually it's less than the course we were about to build.

And sometimes the honest answer is that it isn't a training problem at all. If the issue is motivation, the environment, or something a simple job aid could cover, a course won't fix it.

That's how we like to build custom eLearning: around what people do, not everything we could tell them. The link is in the first comment.
