Alt text: describe the picture in one sentence. Five quick rules for the pictures on your website. Save this for the next time you add a photo.

1. What it's for. A screen reader reads alt text aloud to someone who can't see the picture, and the browser shows it when the picture doesn't load.
2. One sentence. Say who or what is in it, and what's happening. The page around it tells the story.
3. What not to write. Skip "image of" (the screen reader already says it's an image), file names like IMG_2041.jpg, and paragraphs.
4. When to leave it empty. If a picture is only decoration, or only repeats the words right next to it, use an empty alt: alt="". Don't delete the alt entirely.
5. Where to type it. Click the picture in your site editor, open its settings, and find the box called Alt text or Alternative text.
