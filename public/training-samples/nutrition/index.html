Before you build anything

By the end of these eight cards you will be able to put together a balanced plate without measuring anything, find the two lines on a food label that actually change what you buy, and tell the difference between being hungry and being on autopilot.

Nutrition is simply what your food is made of and what your body does with it. That is the whole word. It is not a plan, it is not a rulebook, and it does not come with a list of foods you are banned from.

2

What a balanced plate looks like

The plate method is a way of judging a meal by how the plate is divided rather than by numbers. Open each divider and the plate beside it fills in.

Half the plate

  • Vegetables and fruit bring fibre, water and vitamins, and they take up room on the plate for very little effort.
  • Aim for more than one colour. Different plants carry different things, and colour is the easiest way to vary them without a chart.
  • Frozen and tinned count. So does the bag of salad you did not grow yourself.
  • Cooked counts too. Half a plate of raw salad is a lot of chewing, and roasted vegetables are the same food with the volume turned down.

A quarter of the plate

  • Protein is the part of food your body uses to build and repair things, and it is slow to digest, which is why it keeps you comfortable between meals.
  • Eggs, fish, chicken, beef, yoghurt and cheese all count. So do beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, nuts and seeds.
  • The plant ones bring fibre along with the protein, which is a genuinely useful two for one.
  • A quarter of a plate is roughly the size of your own palm. That is the only measurement in this module.

The other quarter

  • A whole grain is a grain with the whole seed still in it: the bran, the germ and the starchy middle. Milling takes the first two out, and most of the fibre goes with them.
  • Oats, brown rice, wholemeal bread, wholewheat pasta, barley and quinoa are the easy ones to find.
  • Potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn and plantain live here too. They are vegetables in the garden and starch on the plate.
  • Nothing here is forbidden. White rice is food. It simply does less for you than the brown bag next to it.

Beside the plate

  • Fat carries flavour and helps your body absorb some vitamins. Olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado and oily fish are the ones to reach for first.
  • You do not need to measure it. You do need to notice it, because oil and dressing are easy to pour and easy to forget.
  • Water first for drinks. Tea and coffee count towards fluid, whatever you have heard.
  • Sweet drinks are the quietest way to take in added sugars, the sugars put into a food rather than the ones already in the fruit or the milk.

Left half: vegetables and fruit. Top right: protein. Bottom right: grains and starch. Beside it: fats and drinks.

Six lines worth knowing

5

Protein and fibre keep you full

Two things in food slow a meal down on the way through you. That slowness is most of what the word full really describes.

  • Your body takes protein apart and uses the pieces to build and repair muscle, skin and a long list of things you never think about.
  • It leaves the stomach slowly, so a meal with protein in it holds you longer than the same meal without.
  • Spreading it across the day beats saving it all for dinner, and breakfast is where most people are short.

  • Eggs, fish, tinned tuna, chicken, turkey and beef.
  • Yoghurt, milk, cottage cheese and hard cheese.
  • Beans, lentils, chickpeas, split peas, tofu and edamame.
  • Nuts, seeds and the butters made from them: handy, and easy to overpour.

  • Fibre is the part of a plant your body cannot break down. It goes through you largely intact, and that is the point of it.
  • On the way it slows digestion, which softens the rise and fall in your energy, and it adds bulk and water to the plate.
  • It feeds the bacteria living in your gut, and research suggests that community does more for you than we can currently list.

  • Beans and lentils are the cheapest way to add a lot of it at once.
  • Whole grains: oats, brown rice, wholemeal bread, barley.
  • Fruit and vegetables with the skins on, plus nuts, seeds and popcorn.
  • Build it up over a couple of weeks and drink water with it, or it can be uncomfortable.

Hunger or habit

Six ordinary moments. For each one, pick the most likely explanation from what the situation tells you. This is about noticing a cue, not about being right or wrong with your own body.

Moment 1 of 6

Answered 0 of 6.

It is three in the afternoon. You had lunch at noon and you are not thinking about food, but the snack drawer is on the way back from the printer and you have already opened it.

You woke up, had coffee, and it is now mid morning. Your mouth is dry, there is a dull headache behind your eyes, and you have not had a glass of water since yesterday evening.

You slept badly and got up early. By eleven you cannot settle on a task, you keep rereading the same paragraph, and you want something sweet rather than something to eat.

Breakfast was at seven and it is half past twelve. Your stomach is making noise, you have gone slightly short tempered, and a bowl of plain beans and rice sounds genuinely good.

You ate an hour ago. The film is half over, somebody brings the bowl out, and your hand is going back to it without any decision arriving first.

You have just come in from an hour walking in Florida heat. You feel a bit lightheaded, you want something cold, and the thought of a full meal is faintly unpleasant.

Small swaps that stick

Turn a card over to see the swap and why it works. Tick the ones you would actually repeat, and they go onto your card at the end.

0 swaps kept.

  • Instead of

    A fizzy drink with lunch, every day.

    Try

    Sparkling water with a squeeze of lime.

    The cold and the fizz were most of what you wanted. The added sugars were along for the ride.

  • Instead of

    Dry crackers on their own at three o'clock.

    Try

    The same crackers with peanut butter, or a handful of nuts.

    Protein and fat slow the snack down, so four o'clock is quieter than it usually is.

  • Instead of

    White rice, because that is what is in the cupboard.

    Try

    Half brown and half white, cooked in the same pot.

    A swap you will repeat beats a better one you abandon in a fortnight.

  • Instead of

    Deciding about breakfast on the way out of the door.

    Try

    Oats and yoghurt put in a jar the night before.

    A decision made while you are late is not really a decision. Move it to a calmer moment.

  • Instead of

    A salad that arrives already dressed.

    Try

    Dressing on the side, poured by you.

    You keep every bit of the flavour and you get the amount back under your own hand.

  • Instead of

    One vegetable at dinner, if there is one at all.

    Try

    Two, and let one of them be raw and unfussy.

    Raw needs no pan and no attention, which is the only reason it survives a Tuesday.
